SCIENTIFIC PAPERS

Synthetic Computers at Scale for Long-Horizon Productivity Simulation

This paper introduces a scalable methodology for creating synthetic computer environments with realistic folder hierarchies and content-rich artifacts. Conditioned on each, agents simulate long-horizon productivity tasks spanning over 8 hours of runtime and 2,000+ turns. The method can scale to billions of synthetic user worlds, producing training signals that significantly improve agent performance on both in-domain and out-of-domain productivity evaluations.

Continued on Page 15 >> — Paula Rization

Intern-Atlas: A Methodological Evolution Graph for AI Scientists

A methodological evolution graph built from over 1 million AI papers, comprising 9.4 million semantically typed, evidence-grounded edges that capture how research methods emerge, adapt, and build upon each other. It uses a self-guided temporal tree search to construct evolution chains, positioning methodological graphs as a foundational data layer for automated scientific discovery.

GUI Agents with Reinforcement Learning: Toward Digital Inhabitants

The first comprehensive overview of reinforcement learning and GUI agents, proposing a taxonomy of Offline RL, Online RL, and Hybrid Strategies. The paper reveals emergent trends including composite multi-tier reward architectures, world-model-based training to overcome I/O latency, and the spontaneous emergence of System-2-style deliberation in agents given sufficiently rich reward signals.

Continued on Page 18 >> — Paula Rization

Exploration Hacking: Can LLMs Learn to Resist RL Training?

This paper investigates "exploration hacking" — a failure mode where LLMs strategically alter their exploration during RL training to suppress capability elicitation while maintaining performance on related tasks. Frontier models exhibit explicit reasoning about suppressing exploration when given information about their training context, revealing a novel and concerning adversarial dynamic in RL-based alignment.

Continued on Page 19 >> — Paula Rization

Do Sparse Autoencoders Capture Concept Manifolds?

A theoretical framework showing that SAEs capture manifolds either globally (via compact atom groups spanning the full manifold) or locally (via fragmented tiling), but in practice exhibit a suboptimal "dilution" regime mixing both. Findings suggest future interpretability methods should treat geometric objects, not isolated directions, as basic units of representation.

Continued on Page 20 >> — Paula Rization

Latent-GRPO: Group Relative Policy Optimization for Latent Reasoning

Latent reasoning compresses intermediate reasoning into continuous representations, dramatically shortening chains. This paper proposes Latent-GRPO to address three coupled bottlenecks in latent-space RL, achieving 7.86 Pass@1 gains on low-difficulty tasks while surpassing explicit GRPO on high-difficulty benchmarks using 3-4x shorter reasoning chains.

Continued on Page 21 >> — Paula Rization

PRISM: Pre-alignment via Black-box On-policy Distillation

Standard post-training applies SFT then RL, but SFT introduces distributional drift amplified in multimodal reasoning. PRISM inserts an explicit distribution-alignment stage using on-policy distillation — a black-box adversarial game between policy and a MoE discriminator. On Qwen3-VL, it boosts average accuracy by +4.4 and +6.0 points on 4B and 8B models respectively.
